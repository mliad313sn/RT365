## 2. Verdicts

### 2.1 `docs/CAPACITY_MODEL.md` v0.3 — **SIGN WITH FINDINGS (none blocking)**

**In one paragraph, for a non-specialist.** My three blocking findings are closed, and closed properly: not by softening the sentence but by going to the code and saying what it does. F-01 is now three named failure paths with the auto-action each one actually carries, and I checked all three against the catalogue and the alert file line by line. F-03 is now two columns — the component a config file names, and whether anything in the tree emits — and the author's claim that eight of the nine now emit is true: I ran a platform and watched seven of them appear in one process, and read the eighth at its only call site. F-02 is the interesting one, because the author says my finding is no longer true, and he is right: the alert path is now built before any store is opened, and I proved it by corrupting a store on purpose and watching the S1 arrive, which is the abuse test I asked for on 2026-09-08 and could not get then. The two limits he keeps are both real and both correctly stated — I reproduced each — and neither is softened. What I add are three things nobody has stated yet, all discovered by running the system rather than reading it, all non-blocking, and none needing a number: the alert that names a witness problem also fires when the *local* chain is short, an unreadable audit **file** raises the alert whose auto-action is a platform-wide Kill Switch, and a handful of line citations are one or two lines off. The discipline still holds: I re-audited every number in the document and **no number was invented** — the model still contains exactly one filled numeric cell, and it is still the same measured 639 B property of a schema and a fixture.

### 2.2 `docs/DR_PLAN.md` v1.2 — **SIGN WITH FINDINGS — one blocking (D-07, §5 only)**

**In one paragraph, for a non-specialist.** Both of my blocking findings are closed, and D-01 is closed better than I could have asked: I ran the disaster it describes — restored an older audit store and control store against the witness that survived — and the platform came up **halted**, platform-wide, exactly as the new §3 says, with the truncated-chain verdict and the Kill Switch engaged at boot. That sentence is now evidence rather than reasoning, and the ordered restore that prevents it is written down in the order an operator would execute it. D-02 is closed and goes further than my finding did: it says plainly that today the two principals are one, so two-person restore is a procedural control over a single credential until that identity exists. The one thing I cannot sign is the *witness* row of the scenario table, and it is not a goalpost move — it is a row that did not exist in v1.1, describing behaviour that landed in the tree after my last review, and when I ran that behaviour the row turned out to be incomplete in the direction that sends an operator to the wrong artefact: the alert it uses as the trigger for "restore the witness" is also raised when the witness is perfectly intact and the *audit store* is the stale one. The worst thing an operator can do in that state is restore an older witness so that the lengths agree, which is precisely the truncation-laundering this design exists to refuse. One clause fixes it. Two further things I found by running the restore are non-blocking but owed before the drill: the boot attempt itself writes new rows into the chain under suspicion, and the length check the plan asks for in step 2b cannot be performed the way the plan implies.

## 7. Threat-model delta [Committee] — proposed to the Security Architect, who owns THREAT_MODEL

| Candidate | From | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **The alarm names the wrong artefact** | D-07, F-15 | The alert that means "the external witness is in trouble" is also raised when the witness is intact and the local chain is short.
