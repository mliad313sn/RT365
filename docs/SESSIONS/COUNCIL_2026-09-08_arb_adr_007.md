**"One cell per venue cluster" for a tenant with accounts in several countries [Committee].** The council could not make the rule work, and the reason is structural rather than operational:

- The tenant is **not** a deployment unit. `tenant_id` is a server-side principal fact and a predicate on every query (F-15); a tenant is a row-level partition **inside** a cell, and D-043's alternative "cell per tenant" was rejected in ADR-007 itself.
- The tenant's **data** is bound to a residency region (`residency_region`; NFR-PRV-01; D-056: market data does not leave its cell) — a *jurisdiction* axis.
- The tenant's **execution** is bound to a venue — a *market* axis.
- For a tenant with accounts in several countries these two axes **do not coincide**, and ADR-007 provides one unit for both.
- Therefore, under the rule as written, such a tenant is **necessarily present in more than one cell**, and the ADR does not say so, does not say what is authoritative where, and does not say what may be read across.

**FX and the portfolio roll-up across cells [Committee].** The council found that the week's build has already answered the FX half, and that the answer removes rather than adds cross-cell traffic:

- **FX does not need to cross cells.** ADR-021 (proposed, D-064) makes an `FxSnapshot` an input *handed to* the engine and the ledger and never fetched by them; `decide()` does no I/O (F-14). An FX snapshot is therefore an **ingested, cell-local, bitemporal input** exactly like market data, and the correct statement is that FX is *ingested into each cell*, not *called across cells*. A cross-cell FX call would put a synchronous foreign dependency on the decision path of a plane that must never shed and must fail closed — and would be refused by this board for that reason alone. Note that the fail-closed direction is already correct: a missing rate is `RK-FX-MISSING`/`RK-FX-UNDEFINED` and halts; it is never "unlimited" [Source: ADR-021 draft items 5–6]. The FX **licence** per pair and per cell is a human act and remains open [Open: H-08, O-07, O-152, O-153].
- **The portfolio roll-up does need to cross cells, and must be constrained to what it is.** DF-19 already records it as a target (`docs/DATA_FLOWS.md` line 38). The council's constraint: the roll-up is a **read-only, derived, eventually-consistent view**; it is **never** an input to a risk, eligibility or approval decision, because those must remain pure functions of recorded, cell-local inputs (NFR-DET-01: "pure functions of inputs + policy version"; ADR-021's whole reason for existing). A cross-cell number entering `decide()` would break determinism and put a remote dependency on the control plane's critical path. **The roll-up may be *displayed*; it may not *decide*.**

> ### ADR-007 — Regional deployment cell as the failure and residency domain
>
> **Status:** *(filled by the delegate from the Product Owner's decision record; `Proposed` until that entry exists)* · **Date:** 2026-09-07, amended 2026-09-08 · **Deciders:** ARB (advisory, D-039/D-040); the human Product Owner decides · **Consulted:** Enterprise Architect, Cloud Architect, Data Architect, Integration Architect, Security Architect, SRE Lead, counsel-broker-integration, counsel-platform-reliability · **Provenance:** [Committee, derived from 03] / [Committee: ADR-001 planes, ADR-018 control store, ADR-019 trust set, ADR-020 audit anchor, D-043, D-050, D-056, D-058, D-059, D-062; ARB packet docs/SESSIONS/COUNCIL_2026-09-08_arb_adr_007.md] / [Open: H-05 provisioning, Q-11-1 region, O-18 RPO/RTO, O-54 anchor principal, O-160 capacity isolation, R-05 carriers]
>
> Author of the amendment: Enterprise Architect · Reviewer (different line): Cloud Architect · Recommending body: ARB (advisory) · Approver: the human Product Owner (D-039). Raised as O-159 by the Cloud Architect (CA-R1).
>
> ## Context
>
> The platform needs a bounded failure domain and a bounded residency domain. Blueprint 03 sets regional cells with active/standby execution ownership; NFR-PRV-01 and NFR-GLO-01 require residency per tenant region; D-056 places the bitemporal market-data store and its archive inside the tenant's regional cell with no cross-cell market-data replication.
>
> Since this ADR was first drafted the platform has acquired durable state and an external witness, and both bear on where the boundary falls: a durable control-state store, one per service per cell from shadow (ADR-018, D-058, O-115); a durable audit store with an **anchor directory owned by a different principal**, whose absence halts the platform (ADR-020, D-062, O-137); a **public trust set** read by every verifier, including installers and the CLI on operator machines (ADR-019, D-060); and tenant isolation as a server-side principal fact enforced by a predicate on every read and write (D-059, TC-TEN-001..004). A cell is therefore no longer only a route boundary: it is a unit of durable state that must be **restored**, not merely re-routed.
>
> Two clarifications of scope, because both have already caused confusion [Committee]:
>
> - **Noisy-neighbour prevention was named in the original Context and this ADR does not deliver it.** Route isolation between planes is delivered by ADR-001 and tested by TC-NET-001..004. Tenant data isolation is delivered in software and tested by TC-TEN-001..004. **Resource isolation between planes and tenants is delivered by nothing today**: `infra/kubernetes` holds five namespaces and nine network policies and contains no ResourceQuota, LimitRange, PodDisruptionBudget or autoscaler [Open: O-160]. This ADR must not be cited as providing it.
> - **"Cell" is used in this repository with a second, unrelated meaning.** A `JurisdictionCell` is a six-dimension compliance tuple (country × customer type × broker × venue × asset class × feature) whose enablement requires a dual key (NFR-GLO-01, D-050, `services/compliance/compliance_engine/eligibility.py`). It is **not** a deployment unit and nothing in this ADR grants, withholds or affects a market enablement.
>
> ## Decision
>
> 1. **Term.** The unit defined here is the **deployment cell** (written in full at first use in any document). It is distinct from the `JurisdictionCell` of NFR-GLO-01 and D-050, which is a compliance enablement tuple and is never a deployment unit. No document may use the bare word "cell" for a deployment cell where a `JurisdictionCell` could be meant.
>
> 2. **Definition.** A deployment cell is defined by **the residency region it serves**. It contains the five plane namespaces and the plane network policies of ADR-001, and every durable store of the tenants it serves: the control-state store per service (ADR-018, O-115), the bitemporal market-data store and its archive (D-056), and the audit store (ADR-020). The venues a cell's Execution plane may route to are an **attribute** of a cell, not its definition; one cell may serve several venues, and a venue may be served from more than one cell provided each cell holds only its own tenants' data. Residency, not venue proximity, is the partitioning key, because every durable store this platform has is bound to a residency region and none is bound to a venue.
>
> 3. **Venue placement is an open, measured question, not a decision of this ADR.** Whether the Execution plane serving a venue must be placed relative to that venue — for latency, connectivity, session, account-structure or certification reasons — is unevidenced in this repository: no broker, venue or provider document, quote or measurement exists here. It is decided at Gate C on measured baselines and on the broker certification record, never on a date and never on an assumption. [Open: H-05, H-07, O-03, O-32]
>
> 4. **The cell is the failure domain, and the list of what may cross a cell boundary is closed, explicit and directional.** Exactly four items may cross, each with its direction and its property; nothing else may:
>
>    | # | What crosses | Direction | Property it must have |
>    |---|---|---|---|
>    | (i) | **Audit replication** of the audit chain (ADR-020) | outbound from the cell, to a destination outside it | append-only; never a read on the decision path; its lag is the recovery-point input of NFR-DR-01 [Open: O-18] |
>    | (ii) | **Audit anchor publication** — a sealed `ChainHead` (length, head hash, time) and nothing else (ADR-020) | outbound from the cell, to a principal outside it | see item 5; it is a distinct flow from (i) and must not be co-located with it |
>    | (iii) | **Portfolio roll-up** | outbound from each contributing cell into a read model | **read-only, derived and eventually consistent. It is never an input to a risk, eligibility or approval decision**, which remain pure functions of cell-local recorded inputs (NFR-DET-01). It is never on a recovery path. It carries a **residency question** — positions rolled from one region into a view served in another cross a residency boundary — which is the Privacy Lead's and counsel's to answer per jurisdiction, not this board's [Open: O-09, O-10, F-7]. Its carrier is undecided [Open: R-05] |
>    | (iv) | **The public trust set** (ADR-019) | inbound to every cell, and to every verifier outside every cell | public verification material only; it contains no private key and no tenant, market or order data. It is **distributed as a reviewed, signed artefact to every verifier — including installers and the CLI on operator machines — and is therefore not a cell-to-cell dependency**. An absent trust set is an empty trust set and refuses everything (fail closed) |
>
>    **Market data never crosses a cell boundary** (D-056). **No Control-plane or Execution-plane request, and no order, command, decision, approval, limit, lease or Kill Switch state, crosses a cell boundary in either direction.** **FX rates do not cross cell boundaries**: an `FxSnapshot` is ingested into each cell and handed to the engine and the ledger as a recorded input, never fetched by them across a boundary (ADR-021, D-064); a missing, stale or unbudgeted rate halts with `RK-FX-MISSING` / `RK-FX-STALE` / `RK-FX-UNDEFINED` and is never treated as unlimited. Adding a fifth item to the list requires a new ADR and this board; it is never a deployment or a capacity decision, and no throughput, cost or availability argument may add one (CAPACITY_MODEL §0).
>
> 5. **The witness of a cell's audit trail lives outside that cell.** The anchor directory of ADR-020 — the WORM bucket or replica written by a different principal — must be in a **different failure domain** from the cell whose chain it witnesses, and must remain reachable by the standby that takes that cell over. Rationale, and it is the reason this item is in the Decision and not in a runbook: a missing or stale witness fails closed and carries the `killswitch_platform` auto-action (ADR-020, O-137); the documented recovery is restore-then-verify and disabling the check is never the fix (O-134). A witness co-located with the cell it witnesses therefore converts the loss of one region into a **platform-wide halt that cannot honestly be lifted** — the exact blast radius this ADR exists to bound. The deployment identity of that principal remains [Open: O-54].
>
> 6. **Execution ownership is per account, not per cell.** One active executor per account holds a leased lock with a monotonic fencing token (ADR-002, ADR-018); an account is served by exactly one cell at any time. Active/standby is a property of the executor and of the cell; it is not a licence for two cells to hold live orders for one account.
>
> 7. **A tenant is not a deployment unit and may be present in more than one cell.** Tenant isolation is a server-side principal fact enforced by a predicate on every read and write within a cell (D-059). A tenant with accounts in several residency regions is served by several cells; each cell is authoritative for the accounts, orders, decisions and market data it holds; **nothing is authoritative across cells**, and the only cross-cell view of such a tenant is the derived roll-up of item 4(iii).
>
> 8. **What this ADR does not decide.** **It decides no cell plan.** How many cells exist, in which regions, at what size, on which provider, with which storage and node classes, serving which venues, and when any of them is created, is **not decided here**: it is a human act requiring the operating entity's country (Q-11-1), the budget and provider decision (H-05) and the first-cell hypothesis (D-043). **No cell is provisioned today**: `infra/` contains five namespaces, nine network policies, one dev/sim compose file and no other Kubernetes object kind and no IaC module [Open: H-05, A-6, B-15]. **A cell therefore has no capacity, because there is no cell**: no CPU, memory, IOPS, storage, network, throughput, latency or cost figure is stated or implied by this ADR, and none may be derived from it. Nothing in this ADR authorises an environment or advances the environment ladder.
>
> ## Alternatives considered
>
> | Alternative | Why not |
> |---|---|
> | (a) **Single global deployment** | Rejected: unbounded blast radius, and it cannot satisfy residency per tenant region (NFR-PRV-01, NFR-GLO-01) or D-056's rule that market data does not leave its cell |
> | (b) **Cell per tenant** | Rejected on **operational and control grounds**: a deployment unit per tenant multiplies the number of durable stores, audit chains, witnesses and DR drills linearly with tenants, while the isolation it would buy is already delivered and tested in software (D-059, TC-TEN-001..004). *The earlier version of this ADR rejected this alternative on cost. No cost figure exists in this repository and none is quoted; the cost comparison stays* [Open: H-05, O-87] |
> | (c) **Cell per venue cluster** (the earlier decision text) | Rejected as the **definition**, retained as an **attribute** (item 3). "Venue cluster" was never defined anywhere in this repository; it partitions on an axis to which no durable store of this platform is bound; and it forces a tenant with accounts in several countries either to place its data in a venue's region, breaking residency, or to place the Execution plane away from its venue, defeating the rule's own purpose. The latency and connectivity claim implicit in it is unevidenced [Open: H-05, H-07, O-03] |
> | (d) **Cell defined by residency region, venues as an attribute** (chosen) | Chosen: it is the only definition consistent with every durable store the platform has (ADR-018 control store, D-056 market-data store, ADR-020 audit store), with residency per tenant (NFR-PRV-01, NFR-GLO-01), and with a tenant that spans countries |
> | (e) **Defer the whole ADR to Gate C with the provisioning questions** | Rejected: it leaves NFR-RES-01, NFR-DR-01, CONTAINER_DIAGRAM, DR_PLAN and CAPACITY_MODEL resting on a proposal for a further gate (O-159) |
>
> ## Consequences
>
> - **DR is per cell, and a cell's recovery is now dominated by two unmeasured O(n) verifications.** A standby cell must open a control store *and* an audit store, each verifying its own history, before trading reopens; DR_PLAN's order of restoration already puts audit verification ahead of the control and execution planes. RPO and RTO cannot be proposed until these are measured against a stated journal and chain length [Open: O-18, O-111, O-133, O-134].
> - **The witness constraint of item 5 is a DR constraint, not only a security one.** The order of restoration must show the anchor directory as an input the standby reaches, not as something restored from inside the failed cell. DR_PLAN must be amended accordingly.
> - **Cross-cell egress is a permanent cost term of this architecture, not an exception.** Audit replication and anchor publication cross a boundary by design. The storage cost model has no row for it and no measurement [Open: O-161, O-87, H-05]. No figure is stated here.
> - **Residency is simplified for market data and unresolved for the roll-up.** D-056 removes market data from the cross-boundary question entirely; the roll-up puts it back for position data, per jurisdiction [Open: O-09, O-10, F-7].
> - **Noisy-neighbour prevention is not delivered by this ADR** [Open: O-160]; see Context.
> - **A tenant spanning regions has no authoritative global view**, by design. Any product surface that implies one is showing the derived roll-up of item 4(iii) and must be labelled as derived and eventually consistent [Open: UX, O-122].
> - **This ADR creates no infrastructure.** Every "per cell" statement in `docs/CAPACITY_MODEL.md` remains a **shape**, and its numbers arrive only after provisioning (H-05) and the Gate C baselines.
>

## 13. Concerns for the Product Owner

- **PO-1 The ARB recommends deciding ADR-007, but not the sentence you have.** Every member said *amend*; nobody said *decide as proposed*; nobody said *replace*. The reason is that the one-sentence Decision welds two different things together — an **invariant** (the cell is a failure domain; only a named list may cross it) which costs nothing to decide and which five documents genuinely need, and a **provisioning rule** ("one cell per venue cluster") which has no evidence, no owner and no definition. The phrase "venue cluster" is used five times in this repository and **defined nowhere**; every occurrence is a restatement of the ADR's own sentence. I am asking you to decide the invariant and to refuse the provisioning rule, which is why the amended Decision text says, in its own body and not in a footnote, that no cell plan is decided here.

- **PO-2 One defect in the current text could turn a regional failure into a permanent platform halt, and it does not need a provisioned cell to be real.** ADR-020, which you accepted last as D-062, requires the audit trail's witness — the anchor directory — to be owned by a **different principal**, and makes a missing or stale witness fail closed with the platform Kill Switch. ADR-007 never says **where** that witness lives. If it sits inside the cell it witnesses, then losing that region destroys the audited party and its witness in one event; the standby cannot verify the chain; the documented recovery is restore-then-verify, and O-134 rightly forbids disabling the check as the fix. The honest outcome is a platform-wide halt nobody can lift without destroying the evidence. The cell exists to *bound* blast radius and, as written, it can *convert a regional loss into a total one*. This follows from ADR-020's own text, not from a hypothetical. The amendment puts the witness outside the cell as a Decision item, not as a runbook line, because a runbook is not a control.

- **PO-3 The rule "one cell per venue cluster" cannot be made to work for a customer with accounts in more than one country, and the reason is that the architecture has two partitioning axes and one word for both.** Every durable store this platform now has is bound to a **residency region** — the market-data store and its archive (D-056), the control store, the audit store, `residency_region` per tenant. Nothing is bound to a venue. For a tenant trading a venue in another country, the rule forces you to choose between putting the tenant's data in the venue's region (breaking residency) and putting the execution plane away from its venue (defeating the rule's own purpose). Nobody has hit this because there is exactly one cell in the hypothesis (D-043) and it is both things at once. The amendment defines the cell by **residency** and makes venues an **attribute**, with the placement question marked open against a measurement at Gate C. I want to be explicit that no broker, venue or provider document exists in this repository: any claim about where execution must sit relative to a venue is unevidenced, and counsel-broker-integration would not let it stand.

- **PO-4 The word "cell" means two different things in this repository, and one of them has a dual key on it.** A **deployment cell** is infrastructure. A **`JurisdictionCell`** is a six-dimension compliance tuple (country × customer type × broker × venue × asset class × feature) that is a typed, tested object in the code, and NFR-GLO-01 and D-050 say enablement is "per cell by dual key". `docs/PRIVACY_IMPACT.md` records retention "per cell" without saying which. The risk is not that an engineer is confused; it is that a sentence about **provisioning** is read as a sentence about **market enablement**, in a document set where enablement requires two people and a legal record. The amendment writes "deployment cell" in full and says in the ADR that provisioning a deployment cell enables no market and enabling a jurisdiction cell provisions nothing.

- **PO-5 Deciding ADR-007 creates nothing, and I would rather you hear that from me than infer it at Gate C.** `infra/` contains five namespaces, nine network policies, one dev/sim compose file, and no Deployment, StatefulSet, Service, autoscaler, quota, limit range, disruption budget, storage class, volume claim or Terraform module — I verified the inventory independently of the Cloud Architect and it matches his. **A cell has no capacity because there is no cell.** The Cloud Architect made his support for any decision conditional on that sentence appearing in the Decision section itself, and I agreed, because a decided ADR is quoted far more readily than a proposed one. If you decide this, you have decided a **constraint on future work**, not a topology that exists.

- **PO-6 On the "stateless services" requirement, the council is unanimous that the requirement is wrong and the architecture is right.** NFR-SCL-01 was written before this platform had any durable state. It now has a control store, a market-data store, an audit store, an anchor directory, the MCP journals and an undeployed bus — and those stores are precisely why the platform can fail closed across a restart, which you accepted deliberately in D-058 and D-062. Amending the requirement to match a correct architecture is not weakening a control; leaving it would mean the Gate B pack contains a requirement its own capacity model contradicts. Two cautions. First, the Security Architect's: the word "stateless" is doing security work by accident — people read it as "nothing persists, so nothing leaks", and in fact four of these stores hold Confidential data at rest with **no** file permissions, backup, residency or retention today. The replacement text says so. Second: the existing RAID row O-163 and the capacity model each list a different "four" stateful dimensions; the union is six. The replacement text names them with register ids instead of counting them.
