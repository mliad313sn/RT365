## 2. Verdicts

### 2.1 `docs/CAPACITY_MODEL.md` structure v0.2 — **SIGN WITH FINDINGS**

**In one paragraph, for a non-specialist.** I sign this as a *structure*, with findings, three of them blocking my signature until the owner corrects them. What the document was asked to do, it did: it invents nothing. I checked the discipline rather than trying to fill it — there is exactly one filled numeric cell in the whole model (639 bytes for a simulated market snapshot), I reproduced it independently myself and got the same value, all five code parameters match the source by symbol, and the previous version's "replication/egress = 0 by design" — a zero nobody measured, sitting in a cost sheet — was correctly removed before it reached me. That is the hard part and it held. What I cannot sign as written are three statements the document makes about *my* systems, all of which are wrong in the direction that makes an outage worse: it says a lost audit witness halts the platform automatically (the catalogue says it pages a human and deliberately does **not** halt); it presents the start-up integrity checks as fail-closed-and-therefore-visible (they are fail-closed and **silent** — both run before the alert router is connected, so the two loudest failures this platform can have emit no alert and appear only as a process that will not start); and it lists a "measurement point" for nine service levels when only one of the nine actually emits a measurement anywhere in the tree. None of these needs a number to fix. Fix those three and the structure is right, the measurement plan is the right measurement plan, and §4a is the most useful page anyone has written about this platform's recovery behaviour — I have taken it as the basis of §3 and §4 below. Until they are fixed the document would tell a Gate B reader that a control exists which does not, which is the one failure mode a capacity model must not have.

### 2.2 `docs/DR_PLAN.md` v1.1 — **SIGN WITH FINDINGS**

**In one paragraph, for a non-specialist.** The new paragraph is right and I would not soften a word of it: recovery time is now unmeasured because two checks that run at every start-up grow with the platform's own history, a standby cell inherits both, and no honest recovery-time promise can be made until a drill measures them and says how much history it measured against. That is the correct answer and it is more useful than a number would have been. But the plan's **order of restoration** has not been updated to match, and that is what I would be executing at 03:00. Step 2 is the single word "audit" for what is now two stores with an ordering constraint between them, and getting that order wrong does not merely delay recovery — it halts the platform you are restoring, automatically and platform-wide, because a restored audit store that is shorter than the surviving external witness reads as a truncated chain, and a truncated chain carries the Kill Switch. The plan also promises "two-person restore" in a closing sentence without connecting it to the property that two people are there to protect: the whole value of the external witness is that it is written by a *different* principal, and a recovery performed by one operator holding both credentials destroys that property for exactly the window everyone will later want to audit. Two blocking findings, both about the order and the hands, neither about a number.

## 4. The measurement order I would actually run, and which gate needs which

This is the order, not a menu. Each step is a prerequisite for the one below it, and the reason is stated. Nothing here sets a target; every step produces a **baseline** or a **curve**, and a curve is what this platform actually needs, because both start-up costs are functions of history and a single point measured on a small history will be quoted for a large one [Committee].

**Step 0 — Instrument, before measuring anything (prerequisite for every step below and for Gate C entry).**
Eight of the nine SLIs have no emission point (F-03) and one of them cannot be computed at all as currently defined (`alert_delivery_s`, F-03). Measuring before instrumenting produces stopwatch numbers from a test harness and calls them platform baselines. Required: an emission point per SLI at the component named in `slis.yaml`; a **bounded** aggregation window (today's histograms are unbounded lists sorted per query — F-06, so a p99 is neither cheap nor windowed); a timestamp and an acknowledgement on the alert path so `alert_delivery_s` has two ends; the declared `direction:` field so a target can never be compared the wrong way (F-07); and a bootstrap alert sink so start-up failures are not silent (F-02). **Owner: me, with the Backend Lead. Gate: C entry. Nothing below is valid without it.**

**Step 1 — Synthetic, in CI, no environment: the two O(n) curves (prerequisite for O-111, O-133, O-18, and for any sentence containing "restart" or "failover").**
Build a control-store journal of known length and an audit chain of known length at several magnitudes, and measure, at each: control-store `verify()` **wall time and peak RSS**; audit chain verify **wall time and peak RSS**; the audit store's **resident footprint** once loaded; `FileAnchorPublisher._read()` time as a function of anchor count. Produce curves, not points, and state the shape. Costs nothing, needs no cloud account, blocks nothing, and settles the largest unknown in both documents. It is also the only measurement in this list that can be run **today**. **Owner: Performance & Chaos Lead with the Backend Lead. Gate: C (and I would want it before Gate B is convened, because §4a is currently a dependency statement that this step would turn into a shape).**

**Step 2 — Compose topology, single host: history produced per unit of business (prerequisite for converting step 1 into an answer anyone asked for).**
Journal rows and bytes per order lifecycle; audit rows and bytes per order lifecycle and per MCP call; anchor records per unit of activity. Step 1 gives "verify time versus length"; step 2 gives "length versus a day of trading". **Only the two together answer the question a gate will actually ask, which is not "how fast is `verify()`" but "how long does a restart take after ninety days of trading".** Neither step alone is quotable, and quoting step 1 alone is the exact error §4a warns about. **Owner: QA Lead (test id) with the Performance & Chaos Lead. Gate: C.**

**Step 3 — Multi-process on the compose topology (O-117): the in-cell failover composite.**

## 5. Runbook entries owed before shadow

Three fail-closed controls can stop this platform and none of them has a runbook (O-126, O-134, plus the store case that O-134's sibling O-141 and the `execution.store_unavailable` alert imply). I have **written** all three into `docs/INCIDENT_RESPONSE.md` and the deployment-side prerequisites into `docs/DEPLOYMENT_RUNBOOK.md` (see §7 for exactly what I wrote and where). They are recorded here in full so that a different SRE could have written them, and so that the reviewer of those documents — who is not me — can check them against this reasoning [Source: 13].

Each entry follows the same shape, because at 03:00 the shape is the value: **trigger · how you find out (and how you do not) · first action · what you must not do · escalation · evidence to capture before you change anything · exit criteria**.

### RB-11 — Missing or malformed trust set (O-126, O-141)

- **Trigger.** Every asymmetric verification refuses. Signed commands, registry signatures and MCP tool calls fail closed as a group, from the moment a process starts, in a way that looks like a mass credential failure and is not.
- **How you find out.** By the shape of the failure, not by a dedicated alert: a **fleet-wide** refusal that began at a deploy or a restart, with no corresponding change to any credential. `mcp/policies/trust/registry_keys.json` **does not exist in the tree** (O-141): the loader treats absent as empty and refuses everything asymmetric, which is fail-closed by design and indistinguishable at 03:00 from "someone deleted every key". There is no alert whose name says "trust set missing" — add one (SRE-R6).
- **First action.** Confirm the file's presence, ownership, mode and content hash on the affected node, and compare the hash with the release manifest for the running artefact. If it is absent or does not match: this is a **deployment defect, not a security incident** — but you do not get to decide that alone, so page the Security Architect in parallel with the Backend Lead while you check.
- **What you must not do.** Do not disable asymmetric verification, do not fall back to an unsigned path, and do not hand-place a key file from a developer machine. Refusing everything is the control working. The fix is to restore the artefact-shipped trust set and restart; if the trust set is genuinely wrong, the fix is a new signed release, not an edit in place.
- **Escalation.** Security Architect (owner of the trust decision) and Backend Lead (owner of the loader); MCP Security Agent if MCP tool calls are the visible symptom.
- **Evidence before you change anything.** File presence/absence, mode, owner, hash; the release manifest entry; the first refusal's correlation id; the deploy or restart event that precedes it.
- **Exit criteria.** The shipped trust set is in place with the manifest hash, a signed call verifies, and the deployment defect has a ticket. **Rollback of the release is the preferred fix over placing a file by hand** (ROLLBACK_PLAN, "Service release").
- **Owed to close O-126 and not in my gift:** the trust-set file's owner, its packaging step and its review step are a decision for the Security Architect and the Cloud Architect. I have written the *runbook*; the file still does not exist [Open: O-126, O-141].

### RB-12 — Audit witness lost, stale or corrupt (O-134, O-54)

- **Trigger.** `audit.anchor_missing`, S1, reasons `AUD-ANCHOR-MISSING`, `AUD-ANCHOR-STALE`, `AUD-ANCHOR-CHAIN-BROKEN`, `AUD-ANCHOR-TAIL-REMOVED`.
- **How you find out — and the thing to know first.** **This alert does not halt anything.** Its catalogued auto-action is `none`, deliberately: the audit chain itself may be perfectly intact and only the external witness is unavailable (F-01). Trading continues while you work. Equally: the witness is **not** checked when a process starts, so a clean start-up is **not** evidence that the witness is healthy — you must call the published verification explicitly.
- **First action.** (1) Record the incident correlation id from the alert payload **before you touch anything** — the store opens exactly one incident row per process lifetime, and that id is how the eventual `audit.anchor.recovered` row is tied to it. (2) Establish which of the four reasons it is; they have different recoveries. (3) Determine whether the anchor directory is **unreachable** (credential, mount, network to a different principal's store) or **damaged** (chain broken, tail removed) — the first is an access problem, the second is an integrity finding and is a security incident until the Security Architect says otherwise.
- **What you must not do — the whole point of this entry.** **Do not disable the check. Do not re-anchor to "fix" it. Do not restart to clear it.** Recovery is *restore then verify*, never *disable then reopen* (O-134). Publishing a fresh anchor over a chain you have not verified is exactly what restoring a stale backup would look like, and the design refuses it: a scheduled publication may never create the first anchor for a non-empty chain, and only an operator's explicit, audited `seal` may re-anchor. Restarting is worse than useless: the open-incident state is in-process only, so a restart abandons the incident correlation id, the original incident never receives its recovery row, and the next verification opens a **second** incident under a new id (SRE-R3).
- **Escalation.** Security Architect (integrity), Internal Audit (the witness is their control), Cloud Architect (the anchor directory's deployment identity, O-54). If the platform is also halted, this is not RB-12 — go to RB-13.
- **Evidence before you change anything.** Incident correlation id; the four-reason verdict with `length`, `anchor_length`, `lag` and `max_lag` from the payload; a **read-only copy of the anchor file as found**; the last good copy's provenance; who holds the anchor principal's credential and who last used it.
- **Exit criteria.** The anchor directory is restored from the last good copy **by the anchor principal, not by the audit service's identity**, an explicit published verification returns ok, an operator seal is taken and audited, and an `audit.anchor.recovered` row exists **under the original incident correlation id**. If the incident id was lost to a restart, say so in the record rather than closing quietly.
- **Standing gap I cannot close from this seat.** The second principal that owns the anchor directory does not exist yet — it is a deployment identity decision (O-54), and until it does, the "different principal" property that makes this control worth defending is modelled, not evidenced [Open: O-54].

### RB-13 — A store will not open: the platform does not start (ADR-018, ADR-020, O-111, O-134)

- **Trigger.** A process refuses to start. `StoreIntegrityError` (control store journal chain broken, or state does not match the journal), `StoreError` (SQLite unreadable, locked past the 5 s busy timeout, disk gone), or `AuditIntegrityError` (audit chain refused at open).
- **How you find out — read this line twice.** **Not from an alert.** Both verifications run inside the store constructors, which run *before* the alert router is wired (F-02). No `audit.chain_verification_failed` is emitted, no `killswitch_platform` auto-action fires, nothing is delivered, nothing appears on the SLO dashboard. **Your only signal is a process that will not boot**, or, in a cluster, pods that never become ready — so a health-check-based page is what finds this, and the health check is the thing to make sure exists before shadow. Treat "the platform did not come back after a restart" as this runbook until proved otherwise.
- **First action.** (1) **Stop restarting it.** A crash loop on an integrity failure adds nothing and, if the cause is disk or memory pressure, makes it worse. (2) Read the exception text: it names the failure class, and the two classes have opposite recoveries. `StoreError` is an **availability** failure — a disk, a mount, a stuck writer — and is fixed by fixing the environment. `StoreIntegrityError` / `AuditIntegrityError` is an **integrity** failure — the file does not match its own journal or chain — and is a security incident until the Security Architect says otherwise. (3) Take a byte-for-byte copy of both store files and the anchor directory **before** any repair attempt; the file as found is the evidence.
- **What you must not do.** Do not open the store with verification disabled to "get trading back". Do not delete or truncate a journal to make an open succeed. Do not restore a control store without restoring the audit store and the anchor directory as a matched set, and do not restore the audit store to a length below the last published anchor — that reads as a truncated chain and carries `killswitch_platform` platform-wide (D-01). The gateway submitting nothing while the store is unavailable (`execution.store_unavailable`, S1, auto-action `none`) is the control working: no order reaches a broker while this is true.
- **Watch for the amplifier.** If the cause is time or memory rather than corruption — a very long journal, a very long chain — then the restart itself is the problem: `verify()` is O(n) in **time and memory** (F-05), and neither is measured (O-156). A store that cannot finish verifying within the process's memory or the orchestrator's start-up timeout will never open, and no amount of restarting will change that. The lever is more memory and a longer start-up budget **now**, and compaction/retention **later** (O-111, O-133) — which are integrity and legal decisions, not SRE tuning knobs (O-135).
- **Escalation.** Backend Lead (store implementation), Security Architect (any integrity verdict), Cloud Architect (disk, node, restore), Chief Risk Agent before any account resumes trading, Compliance if the audit trail is affected. Incident command per INCIDENT_RESPONSE; this is S1.
- **Evidence before you change anything.** Exception class and message including the failing sequence number; copies of `control_state` and `audit_state` store files and the anchor directory as found; the journal length, chain length and anchor count at failure (these are the numbers the drill record will later be compared against — F-11); the last clean start of the same process; disk, memory and start-up-timeout state at the moment of failure.
- **Exit criteria.** The store opens and verifies from a restored copy; the audit chain verifies **against the witness** (explicitly — the open-time verify does not check it); reconciliation against broker statements has run before any account resumes; the resumption is a two-person act; and the post-incident review records the three history lengths and the four-phase timing, so that the incident becomes an O-18 input instead of an anecdote.

### RB-14 — The DR drill record (D-04, F-11) — a requirement, not an incident

A drill that does not state what it ran against is not evidence, and this is the single most quotable artefact this project will produce. Every DR drill record must state, as a precondition of being cited anywhere:

1. **Control-store journal rows** at drill time.
2. **Audit chain events** at drill time.
3. **Anchor records** at drill time (they grow at 1/`anchor_every` of the event rate plus one per seal, so they are not derivable from 2).
4. The **wall-clock split**: restore · control-store `verify()` · audit-chain verify · anchor verify · reconciliation-before-reopen. A single total conceals which of the four to attack.
5. **Peak resident memory** during each verification (F-05) — the term that decides whether a big restore completes at all.
6. The **environment and disk class** it ran on, and the explicit statement that none is chosen yet (H-05, §1a).
7. Whether the cell was **fresh or aged**, and if fresh, the sentence *"this is a best case and may not be quoted as a general recovery time"* in the record itself, not in a covering note.
8. **Who acted**: the identities that restored the audit store and the anchor directory, and whether they were different people (D-02).

A drill missing any of 1–3 or 7 produces a number I will refuse to carry into O-18 [Committee].

## 10. Concerns for the Product Owner

- **PO-S1 The two worst things that can happen to this platform currently page nobody.** Two integrity checks run every time a process starts: the control store replays its journal, and the audit store verifies its chain. Both are correct, and I would not weaken either. But both run *inside* the code that opens the store, and the alerting system is connected a few lines *later* — so when either fails, the catalogued S1 alert is not emitted, the automatic Kill Switch does not fire, nothing is delivered to a pager and nothing appears on a dashboard. What you actually get is a service that does not come back. I have written the runbook to say exactly that, because it is true today and someone will meet it at 03:00; but a runbook is a workaround, not a fix. Before this platform runs in shadow I want the alert path connected before the stores are opened, and an abuse test that corrupts a journal on purpose and proves an S1 arrives. Until then, "fail closed" is accurate and "fail closed and you will be told" is not (SRE-R1) [Committee].

- **PO-S2 The most likely mistake during a disaster recovery is one that halts the platform you are recovering.** The audit trail is witnessed by an external record of how long it is. If a standby cell is brought up with an audit store *shorter* than that witness — which is what restoring an older backup, or a partial restore, looks like — the platform correctly concludes the trail has been truncated, and a truncated trail carries an automatic platform-wide Kill Switch. So the ordinary DR error does not cost you minutes; it stops trading everywhere, during an incident, for a reason that will look like a second disaster. The fix is free and is an ordering instruction, not a number: restore the audit store, check its length against the witness, *then* attach the witness, then verify explicitly, then seal. It is not in the DR plan today and I have asked the Cloud Architect to put it there before the plan goes any further (D-01) [Committee].

- **PO-S3 Recovery is the moment the audit witness stops being independent, unless you require two people.** The reason the external witness is worth having is that changing the audit trail would require the cooperation of two separate identities. A restore performed by one operator holding both sets of credentials removes that property — for precisely the window that anyone investigating the incident will later care most about. The DR plan says "two-person restore" in its last sentence and never connects it to this. I want the anchor directory named as its own restore unit, with its own credential, restored by a different person, and both names written into the recovery record; and if one person did hold both, I want that stated rather than quietly true. I would rather explain an awkward record than discover the control was hollow when it mattered (D-02, O-54) [Committee].

- **PO-S4 We can measure almost none of the nine service levels today, and that is not the capacity model's fault — it is mine.** The capacity model lists a measurement point for each of the nine service-level indicators, taken faithfully from the observability configuration. Those entries name a *component*, not a measurement: exactly one of the nine actually emits anything anywhere in the code — time-to-halt. There is no counter for control-plane availability, no observation of data freshness or decision latency, and *alert delivery time cannot be computed at all*, because the alert path records no timestamp and there is no acknowledgement and no notification component. I am not asking you to fund a tool; I am telling you that instrumenting must come **before** the baseline programme, or every "baseline" you are shown at Gate C will have been produced by a stopwatch in a test harness and will be quoted as a property of the platform. I have put instrumentation as step 0 of the measurement order and made it a Gate C entry condition (F-03, SRE-R4, SRE-R5) [Committee].

- **PO-S5 The discipline you asked for held, and I checked it rather than helped it.** My instruction was not to fill cells and not to accept a number nobody measured. The capacity model contains exactly one filled numeric cell in the whole document. I reproduced it myself, independently, and got the same value that the review board measured and the Independent Validation Agent reproduced — three independent reproductions of one number, and it is still only a property of a data schema and a test fixture, not of any market feed, venue, broker or deployed system. I also re-checked all five values the document quotes out of the source code and they match. And I want you to know what was caught on the way here: an earlier draft carried a cost of "zero" for cross-region data transfer that nobody had measured or been quoted, in a sheet whose own rule forbids exactly that; the Cloud Architect removed it before it reached me. A zero is the number least likely ever to be questioned. The system of author, owner and separate reviewer worked on this document, three times over, and it is the reason I can sign anything at all (F-12) [Committee].

- **PO-S6 I cannot tell you how quickly this platform recovers, and I would rather say so than give you a number.** Recovery time now depends on how much history the platform has, because both integrity checks read all of it, and a standby cell must do both before trading can reopen. That is the right design and I am not asking to change it. What it means for you: the honest answer to "how fast do you recover an account, or a region" is *unmeasured*, and it will stay unmeasured until a drill runs and states how much history it ran against. A drill on a fresh system measures the best case and will be quoted as the general case — so I have written into the drill requirements that a record must state three separate history lengths, the time split across four phases, the peak memory, and, if the system was fresh, the sentence saying so. I have also added the term the model did not have: these checks are limited by **memory** as well as by time, and a restore too large to fit in memory does not recover slowly, it does not recover at all. There is no memory limit configured anywhere to size that against (F-05, F-11, SRE-R9, O-160) [Open: O-18].

- **PO-S7 One decision I would like taken deliberately rather than inherited.** A broken audit chain automatically halts trading platform-wide. That is the largest automatic consequence in this system, it is the right direction for a control envelope, and nobody has actually decided it — it arrived with a build. It is already logged as needing the Trading Risk Committee's confirmation (O-137), and I support the direction; I simply do not want the first time it is examined to be the first time it fires. Related and smaller: the parameter that controls how often the audit trail is witnessed is currently a placeholder, and I have found that it is not only a security setting — it is also a divisor of a cost the platform pays continuously, so the security board and the capacity work must decide it together rather than in sequence (F-04, SRE-R7, O-133) [Open: O-137].

I wrote three runbooks and two operational documents, and I have **not** reviewed them — the Security Architect and the Backend Lead are their reviewers of record and both are pending.
