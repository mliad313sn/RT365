# ADR-020 — Durable audit on the store seam, witnessed by an external anchor written by a different principal

**Status:** Accepted for dev/sim with conditions (D-062, 2026-09-08; conditions (a) the anchor principal becomes a real identity with its own write credential before shadow — O-54; (b) cadence and staleness ceiling are placeholders decided with the retention rules — O-133; (c) the runbook carries witness recovery before shadow — O-134; (d) retention, legal hold and erasure against an append-only chain go to the Privacy Lead and Compliance — O-135; (e) the platform-wide Kill Switch on a chain failure is confirmed deliberately by the Trading Risk Committee — O-137). Amended in effect by D-066 (2026-09-08: composition calls the published-anchor verification at start-up; O-164, O-165). **The decision-log entry is the truth; this line quotes it and does not create it** [Source: 13, CLAUDE.md] · **Date:** 2026-09-08 · **Deciders (ARB, Security & Privacy Board):** ARB, Security & Privacy Board · **Consulted:** SRE Lead, Security Architect, Backend Lead, Independent Validation Agent, Privacy Lead · **Provenance:** [Source: 03 stack, 06 immutable logs] / [Committee: ADR-018 seam, REVIEW_C5 OBJ-3, IVA-09, D-058, Security & Privacy Board recommendation COUNCIL_2026-09-08_gate_B_spb_docs §3.1] / [Open: O-54 anchor principal, O-110 store integrity, O-111 compaction, O-121 platform auditor]

Author: build agent E11 (AI), completed by the Product Owner delegate · Reviewer: pending (SRE Lead, Security Architect) · Approver: pending (ARB and Security & Privacy Board recommend; the Product Owner decides, D-039/D-040). Register item B-5, delegation D-051.

## Context

ADR-018 made control state durable and D-058 accepted it for dev/sim. That left an inconsistency an auditor would find immediately: **control state survived a restart while the audit trail of what happened to it did not.** The audit store was an in-process list with an optional JSONL file; R-31 recorded that the chain could not detect tail truncation and that `seal()` anchors were produced in-process (IVA-09), which is to say the audited party was also the witness.

The Security & Privacy Board, asked to choose the integrity scheme for the durable control store (O-110), recommended anchoring the control store's journal head into the audit chain, whose own head is anchored off-box by a different principal, in preference to a keyed HMAC — because a key inside the Execution plane is a secret to custody there, and an attacker who can read the process reads the key anyway. That recommendation only pays if the audit chain is itself durable and externally witnessed. This ADR builds that.

## Decision

**Three layers, each catching what the layer below cannot.**

1. **The seam.** Audit events are rows on `rtcore.store` (ADR-018): per-row digests and a hash-chained journal, so an edited or deleted row in the file is refused when the store is opened. The audit trail gets **its own store file** (`audit_state.sqlite`), never the control store's: control state and the evidence of what happened to it do not share a file, a transaction domain or a restore unit.
2. **The audit chain.** Each event keeps `prev_hash`/`hash` over its own fields and `payload_hash` over its payload, verified when the store is opened and refused with `AuditIntegrityError` on any break. This survives the offline attacker O-110 admits: someone who rewrites rows *and* recomputes every unkeyed seam digest still cannot produce a consistent audit chain.
3. **The external anchor.** `AnchorPublisher` receives a sealed `ChainHead` — a length, a head hash and a time — and nothing else. `FileAnchorPublisher` writes them to a directory the audit process does not own (`anchor_dir`; a WORM bucket or replica written by a different principal in deployment) as append-only records forming *their own* hash chain, so an edited, removed or inserted anchor record is detected. Truncating the audit trail now requires write access to two stores owned by two principals.

**Rules that follow, and are tested:**

- **The sequence is allocated by the store**, not by the length of an in-memory list, so what a restart reads is what was durably allocated.
- **No update and no delete path exists** at any layer, for any caller. The publisher and the `AnchorHandle` it may hold expose exactly `head` and `length`.
- **The witness is never allowed to contradict itself.** A publication whose head disagrees with the last published anchor is refused, and a *scheduled* publication may never create the first anchor for a non-empty chain — a fresh anchor over an unwitnessed chain is precisely what restoring a stale backup would produce. Only an operator's explicit `seal` may re-anchor, and that seal is itself audited.
- **An empty chain is anchored at length 0** (genesis), so "no anchor" can never be read as "nothing has happened yet".
- **Verification fails closed and stays failed.** A truncated tail, a head mismatch, a missing anchor, a broken anchor chain, a removed anchor tail and an anchor staler than the configured lag each have a reason code, raise the catalogued S1 alert (`audit.chain_verification_failed` or `audit.anchor_missing`), and open **exactly one** incident row until the chain verifies again — at which point a `audit.anchor.recovered` row closes it under the same correlation id.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keyed HMAC over the audit rows.** Rejected for the reason the Board gave for the control store: it adds a secret to custody and rotate, and it fails against an actor who can read the process. The external witness defends against a strictly larger class of attacker without a new secret.
- **A transparency log (RFC 6962 style) with a Merkle tree and inclusion proofs.** The right answer at scale and the natural successor: inclusion and consistency proofs would let a third party verify a single event without the whole chain. Rejected for now as disproportionate to dev/sim volumes and to the number of verifiers; the anchor record is deliberately shaped so a Merkle root can replace the head hash without changing the seam. [Open]
- **A public blockchain anchor.** Rejected: it publishes a fingerprint of internal activity timing to an uncontrolled audience, costs money per anchor, and the trust it adds over a WORM store written by a different principal is small for a private trading platform.
- **Signed timestamps from a TSA.** Complementary rather than alternative; it proves *when*, not *what was not removed*. Worth adding to the anchor record once a TSA exists. [Open]
- **Keep the JSONL file and back it up.** Rejected: a backup proves nothing about what was in the file before the backup ran, which is the OBJ-3 finding.

## Consequences

- The audit trail now survives a restart, and a restore of a stale backup is detectable rather than silent.
- Two directories must be provisioned, owned by two identities, and both must be in the DR plan. Who that second principal is in deployment stays O-54; the file publisher models it, it does not evidence it.
- Anchor records accumulate: one per explicit seal and one per `anchor_every` events. Compaction and retention are owed with the control-store journal's (O-111).
- A lost witness is now a fail-closed condition. That is the intended behaviour, and it is also a new way to stop a deployment: the runbook must say that restoring the replica, not disabling the check, is the fix.
- The cadence (`anchor_every`, `max_anchor_lag`) bounds how many events a consistent-rewrite attacker could reach and how stale the witness may be. Both defaults are dev/sim placeholders; the real values come from the O-54 decision and the retention rules. [Open]
- The Kill Switch auto-action on `audit.chain_verification_failed` means a broken chain halts the platform. That is deliberate and it is drilled by TC-AUD-007.

## Controls and tests affected

| Control | Test |
|---|---|
| Events, seals and anchors survive a rebuild; verify against the published anchor; tenant-partitioned reads unchanged; memory default unchanged | TC-AUD-006 (positive) |
| Consistent rollback invisible to the seam is caught by the anchor; stale anchor beyond the ceiling; missing anchor; no silent re-anchoring after a restart | TC-AUD-007 (negative) |
| Edited row refused by the seam and, after a consistent digest rewrite, by the chain; edited, shortened and mid-removed anchor files; publisher and handle expose no audit write path; rollback and fork anchors refused | TC-AUD-008 (abuse) |

# Amendment 2 — the alert path exists before any store is opened, and the published witness is verified at start-up

**Status:** Proposed (2026-09-08) · **Amends:** §Decision rule 5 ("Verification fails closed and stays failed") and the §Consequences line on the Kill Switch auto-action · **Deciders:** the Product Owner decided the start-up verification (D-066); ARB and the Security & Privacy Board recommend on ADR-020 as a whole (D-039/D-040) · **Consulted:** SRE Lead (author of finding F-02 / SRE-R1 and of runbook RB-13), Security Architect, Backend Lead, Cloud Architect · **Provenance:** [Committee: D-066, O-164; REVIEW_2026-09-08_capacity_sre.md §3 F-02, §6, §10 PO-S1; ADR-018 amendment 1 §5–§6] / [Open: O-54, O-134, O-137, O-133] · Register item E-6, delegation D-051. Author: build agent E11 (AI). Reviewer: pending (Compliance Agent 2nd line, SRE Lead as the finding's author, Security Architect). Approver: pending. Written with the commit that introduced the mechanism; it does not approve it.

**Why an amendment and not a new ADR.** Rule 5 of the original Decision says a verification failure "raise[s] the catalogued S1 alert". For the two verifications that run when a store is *opened* that sentence was not true of the running system: the composition root built the stores and wired `set_alert_sink` a few lines later, so `AuditStore.__init__` ran its chain verification with `self._alerts is None` and `SqliteStore.__init__` ran `verify()` before any router existed. The failures with the largest blast radius on this platform emitted nothing — no S1, no auto-action, no delivery — and the only operator signal was a process that would not come back (F-02). That is a correction to a rule this ADR already states, so it belongs in this file, where an auditor following the rule will look. D-066's start-up verification is the same sentence extended to the *published* witness, which is why the two land together.

## Decision

1. **The alert router is built before any store is opened.** `build_sim_platform` loads `observability/alerts.yaml` as its first act and passes `alerts.raise_alert` into every store open. A caller may hand in its own router (`alert_router=`) so that a supervisor, or a deployment whose pager exists before the platform does, can receive the start-up verdict; it must be a router loaded from the catalogue, because severity and auto-action are the catalogue's to state, never the caller's.
2. **Every store open is announced before it propagates.** A durable store is opened as schema-then-`verify()` with the sink in place. A refusal raises the catalogued S1 carrying the start-up `correlation_id`, the reason code, the failing detail, the store file and `phase: open`, and is then **re-raised unchanged**. Alerting is a notification, never permission: the platform still fails closed and never composes. Nothing is caught and swallowed, and no `verify` is skipped — `verify=False` on the constructor only moves the same check one line out, so that the failure has somewhere to be announced.
3. **Which alert, and why that one.** The control store → `execution.store_unavailable` (S1, auto-action `none`: an automatic write to a store under investigation would destroy the evidence, the reasoning ADR-018 amendment 1 §5 already gives). The audit store, at the seam or on its own chain → `audit.chain_verification_failed` (S1, auto-action `killswitch_platform`): the evidence store did not open, which is the largest blast radius this platform has. No new alert name is introduced: adding one to `observability/alerts.yaml` without the matching row in `docs/ALERT_CATALOG.md` — which this ADR's author may not write — would make the two files disagree, and the catalogue is the source of truth for what an alert does.
4. **Two reason codes, because two recoveries.** `STORE-OPEN-REFUSED` (integrity: a row, the journal, or the replay against the state does not match) and `STORE-OPEN-UNAVAILABLE` (availability: the file, disk or mount could not be read at all). RB-13 turns on this distinction: the first is a suspected tampering incident and the files as found are the evidence; the second is an environment fault. An operator who cannot tell them apart from the page will guess.
5. **One correlation id per start-up attempt.** `startup:<id>` is stamped on every alert of that attempt, including the audit chain's own, so the control-store refusal and the audit refusal of one failed boot are one incident and not two. An already-open anchor incident keeps its own id, so an incident is never split (ADR-020's "exactly one incident row" is unchanged).
6. **The auto-action of a start-up failure may be unrunnable, and that is said out loud.** At open there is no platform to halt, so `killswitch_platform` is unbound and the router raises `alert.autoaction_failed` (S1) naming it. The operator is told both that the chain failed and that the automatic halt did not run. No no-op is bound in its place: a halt that did not happen must not be reported as one, and the process refusing to start is a stronger stop than the Kill Switch it could not reach.
7. **D-066: composition verifies the *published* witness at start-up.** After the auto-actions are bound, `build_sim_platform` calls `audit.verify()` (published) when an anchor publisher is configured, and keeps the verdict on `SimPlatform.startup_verification`. On failure it follows this ADR's existing path — the catalogued S1 and its auto-action, up to the platform Kill Switch — and **does not refuse to compose**: it runs at that point precisely because a composition that refused could not run the halt the failure calls for, and the halt is the stronger control. The cheap journal-head comparison of ADR-018 amendment 1 is blind to an attacker who rewrites both stores consistently (TC-DUR-007 (d)); until now nothing asked the external anchor until the next scheduled publication, an explicit `/v1/audit/verify` or `rt365 check`, and the platform traded in the meantime.
8. **Nothing here weakens a check, and nothing here is a new capability.** No verification was removed, relaxed or made optional; there is still no flag to disable one (O-165). No AI/MCP component gains a path: the router, the store opens and the start-up verdict are composition-root code, unreachable from any tool handler, and the audit store still has no update and no delete API at any layer.

## Consequences

- **Rule 5 is now true of the running system.** A store refused at open raises, delivers and correlates its catalogued S1. TC-AUD-011 and TC-AUD-012 assert on what the router **delivered**, not on the sink having been called.
- **A start-up failure now produces two S1s**, the failure and `alert.autoaction_failed`. That is the intended, honest shape; alert-fatigue tuning is the SRE Lead's, and it must not be tuned by making the second one disappear.
- **Every durable start-up now pays a published-anchor read** (`FileAnchorPublisher._read()` re-verifies the whole anchor file) on top of the chain load and verification that were already O(events) in time and memory. This is a start-up cost that grows with history and with anchor count; it belongs in the O-156 measurement with the other two (F-04, F-05, F-11), and `anchor_every` (O-133) is one of its divisors.
- **A missing or stale witness is now noticed at start-up** and pages, with auto-action `none` — so a platform whose witness is gone starts, trades and pages, exactly as `audit.anchor_missing` is catalogued. A platform whose chain **contradicts** the witness starts **halted** by `killswitch_platform`. These are two different outcomes of one check and the runbooks and the capacity model must say which is which (F-01).
- **The DR order of restoration matters more, not less.** D-066 moves the truncated-chain verdict from "some time after the platform started" to "before it trades", so the most natural DR error (an audit store shorter than the surviving witness) now halts the standby at start-up. That is the right time to learn it, and it makes DR_PLAN D-01's ordered restore a prerequisite rather than an improvement.
- **`SimPlatform.startup_verification`** gives `rt365 check`, the BFF dashboard and an operator one place to read what the platform concluded about itself at boot. It is a verdict, not a permission: nothing consults it to decide whether to trade.
- **Unchanged:** memory platforms (no publisher, no start-up verdict, byte-for-byte the same alerts), every event schema, the absence of any update or delete path, and the modelled adversary of TC-AUD-007 and TC-DUR-007.

# Amendment 3 — a platform that had a witness and no longer has one does not open, and no publication witnesses a chain below the floor

**Status:** Proposed (2026-09-08) · **Amends:** §Decision rule 3 ("The witness is never allowed to contradict itself"), rule 5 ("Verification fails closed and stays failed") and the §Consequences line "A lost witness is now a fail-closed condition" · **Decider:** the Product Owner (2026-09-08, on the Red-Team Lead's Case B and Case C; D-039 authority). ARB and the Security & Privacy Board recommend on ADR-020 as a whole · **Consulted:** Red-Team & Pen-Test Lead (author of the probe), SRE Lead, Security Architect, Backend Lead · **Provenance:** [Committee: Product Owner decision 2026-09-08; D-066; `docs/PENTEST/probes/rt_probe_01_audit_witness.py` Cases B and C; ADR-020 amendment 2] / [Open: O-54, H-21, O-110, R-31] · Register item E-6, delegation D-051. Author: build agent E11 (AI). Reviewer: pending (Compliance Agent 2nd line, Red-Team Lead as the finding's author, SRE Lead). Approver: pending. Written with the commit that introduced the mechanism; it does not approve it.

**Why an amendment and not a new ADR.** Rule 3 of the original Decision says the witness is never allowed to contradict itself, and the §Consequences say a lost witness "is now a fail-closed condition". Measured on 2026-09-08, neither was true of the running system:

* **Case B.** Delete `audit_anchors.jsonl`, restart: *"the platform STARTED without a witness: 61 events, latest anchor=None … an intent ran: final_state=FILLED, broker submissions=1"*. The S1 `audit.anchor_missing` fired four times with `auto_action: none`, and nothing stopped. Fail-closed detection with an auto-action of none is not a fail-closed condition; it is a page.
* **Case C.** Truncate the chain, delete the witness, let an operator seal: *"after a plain operator seal: published anchor length=56, verify() ok=True … the truncation is now witnessed as if it had never happened"*. The one action available to an operator whose alert says the witness is missing destroyed the evidence the witness existed to protect.

Both are corrections to rules this ADR already states, so they belong in this file.

## Decision

1. **Refusal, not a Kill Switch.** A platform whose audit store's own records name an external witness, and whose anchor store now holds no readable record, **does not open**: `AuditStore` raises `WitnessLostError`, and composition fails closed exactly as it does for a control store whose head cannot be confirmed. The auto-action of `audit.anchor_missing` stays `none` and D-066 stands: a Kill Switch activation is itself a store row, and reacting to a store under suspicion by writing to it destroys evidence. The refusal is the stop; the alert is the notification. **Nothing is written on this path** — no incident row, no publication, no activation.
2. **The genesis case is distinguished honestly.** A chain that has never been witnessed at all — no seal record naming an anchor, no publication row — is ADR-020's existing genesis case and still opens. What is refused is a chain whose own records say a witness existed. The two are different findings and they have different runbooks.
3. **The store records every publication it makes, in two places, because they fail differently.** An operator `seal` is recorded as a `SealRecord` with its length, head hash and anchor sequence. A genesis or scheduled publication is recorded as an `audit.anchor.published` row **in the hash chain itself**. This is not redundancy: it was measured that the offline rollback of Case C removes the seal records with the events, because a seal is written after the events it seals — so the seal ledger alone cannot carry "this store had a witness" through the attack. The genesis mark is the first event of the chain and cannot be removed without breaking the chain at sequence 0. Only anchor numbers enter the chain — a sequence, a length, a head hash, a principal — never a payload and never agent-supplied text (F-15).
4. **No publication may witness a chain below the floor.** The floor is the set of heads this store recorded witnessing. Before *any* publication — an operator seal, a scheduled one, or the attested act — the chain is checked against every one of them; a contradiction is refused with `AUD-SEAL-BELOW-FLOOR` and the catalogued S1 `audit.chain_verification_failed`, whatever the external witness says or does not say, and nothing is published. It is checked before automatic publications too, and not only before an operator's seal, because a scheduled publication that re-anchored a truncated-and-refilled chain would launder it with nobody in the room at all.
5. **An ordinary seal may no longer create the first anchor for a non-empty chain** (`AUD-SEAL-UNWITNESSED`). ADR-020 rule 3 said "only an operator's explicit `seal` may re-anchor"; that sentence is what Case C used. Re-anchoring is now a different operation with a different name.
6. **Re-establishment is an explicit, attested, audited operator act.** `audit_service.reestablish` offers exactly two operations: a **read-only** `inspect_chain` (no `AuditStore` is opened, nothing can be written through what it returns), and `reestablish_witness`, which is refused unless the witness really is absent and readable-as-absent, the attestation states the actor, a reason, and *exactly* the chain length, head hash and last recorded anchor sequence, and the chain clears the floor. It writes one `audit.witness.reestablished` row under the operator's correlation id, then publishes an anchor over the head **that includes that row**, so the act witnesses itself. A repeat is refused (`AUD-WITNESS-PRESENT`). There is no flag, no environment variable and no composition-root parameter that reaches it: `build_sim_platform` has no `attestation` parameter, and a test asserts it.
7. **Nothing here can be disabled, and nothing here is a new capability.** No verification was removed, relaxed or made optional (O-165 unchanged). No AI/MCP component gains a path: the checks are inside the audit store's constructor and publication path, the operator act is a library entry point outside the composition root, and there is still no update and no delete API at any layer. No new alert name was introduced — `audit.anchor_missing` already reads "absent, stale beyond the ceiling, broken or ahead of the published witness", and a chain contradicting its own record is a hash mismatch — so `observability/alerts.yaml` and `docs/ALERT_CATALOG.md` still agree.
8. **Anchor sequences are witness-relative; chain lengths are not.** After a re-establishment the new witness numbers its records from 1, so `recorded_anchor_seq` counts only publications made since the last `audit.witness.reestablished` row. Lengths recorded before it stay in the floor for ever.

## Consequences

- **A witness-store outage is now a platform outage.** That is the intended trade and it must be in the runbook and in the capacity/availability model: the anchor volume becomes a start-up dependency of the same rank as the control store. RB-13's restore-then-verify is the first recovery; the attested act is for when there is nothing left to restore.
- **A start-up that fails this check produces one S1 and no auto-action**, by design. An operator who sees `AUD-WITNESS-LOST` is being told the chain may well be intact and that the missing thing is the witness.
- **Two new start-up costs**: reading the anchor store's latest record (already paid since amendment 2) and scanning the chain's publication rows. Both are O(events) on data already in memory; they belong in the O-156 measurement.
- **The chain grows by one row per genesis and per scheduled publication** (one per `anchor_every` events, plus one at birth). This is the compaction question O-111 already owns.
- **`AUD-ANCHOR-TAIL-REMOVED` is now computed from the marks as well as the seals**, so trimming the witness's tail is detected even when the store's seal ledger did not survive.
- **Unchanged**: memory platforms (no publisher, no marks, no floor, byte-for-byte the same behaviour), every event schema, the absence of any update or delete path, and the modelled adversary of TC-AUD-007/008 and TC-DUR-007.
- **What this does not close** (for the Product Owner, not buried): an actor who rewrites the *whole* chain from genesis and deletes the witness leaves a store with no evidence it was ever witnessed, and it will open; an actor who deletes both stores gets a clean slate (probe 05); and the attested act is itself available to an actor who owns both stores — what it costs them is a row in the chain naming them. All three need a witness the audit process cannot write or delete, which is H-21/O-54, not this file.
