# ADR-020 — Durable audit on the store seam, witnessed by an external anchor written by a different principal

## Context

ADR-018 made control state durable and D-058 accepted it for dev/sim. That left an inconsistency an auditor would find immediately: **control state survived a restart while the audit trail of what happened to it did not.** The audit store was an in-process list with an optional JSONL file; R-31 recorded that the chain could not detect tail truncation and that `seal()` anchors were produced in-process (IVA-09), which is to say the audited party was also the witness.

The Security & Privacy Board, asked to choose the integrity scheme for the durable control store (O-110), recommended anchoring the control store's journal head into the audit chain, whose own head is anchored off-box by a different principal, in preference to a keyed HMAC — because a key inside the Execution plane is a secret to custody there, and an attacker who can read the process reads the key anyway. That recommendation only pays if the audit chain is itself durable and externally witnessed. This ADR builds that.

## Decision

**Three layers, each catching what the layer below cannot.**

1. **The seam.** Audit events are rows on `rtcore.store` (ADR-018): per-row digests and a hash-chained journal, so an edited or deleted row in the file is refused when the store is opened. The audit trail gets **its own store file** (`audit_state.sqlite`), never the control store's: control state and the evidence of what happened to it do not share a file, a transaction domain or a restore unit.
2. **The audit chain.** Each event keeps `prev_hash`/`hash` over its own fields and `payload_hash` over its payload, verified when the store is opened and refused with `AuditIntegrityError` on any break. This survives the offline attacker O-110 admits: someone who rewrites rows *and* recomputes every unkeyed seam digest still cannot produce a consistent audit chain.
3. **The external anchor.** `AnchorPublisher` receives a sealed `ChainHead` — a length, a head hash and a time — and nothing else. `FileAnchorPublisher` writes them to a directory the audit process does not own (`anchor_dir`; a WORM bucket or replica written by a different principal in deployment) as append-only records forming *their own* hash chain, so an edited, removed or inserted anchor record is detected. Truncating the audit trail now requires write access to two stores owned by two principals.

**Rules that follow, and are tested:**

- **The sequence is allocated by the store**, not by the length of an in-memory list, so what a restart reads is what was durably allocated.
- **No update and no delete path exists** at any layer, for any caller. The publisher and the `AnchorHandle` it may hold expose exactly `head` and `length`.
- **The witness is never allowed to contradict itself.** A publication whose head disagrees with the last published anchor is refused, and a *scheduled* publication may never create the first anchor for a non-empty chain — a fresh anchor over an unwitnessed chain is precisely what restoring a stale backup would produce. Only an operator's explicit `seal` may re-anchor, and that seal is itself audited.
- **An empty chain is anchored at length 0** (genesis), so "no anchor" can never be read as "nothing has happened yet".
- **Verification fails closed and stays failed.** A truncated tail, a head mismatch, a missing anchor, a broken anchor chain, a removed anchor tail and an anchor staler than the configured lag each have a reason code, raise the catalogued S1 alert (`audit.chain_verification_failed` or `audit.anchor_missing`), and open **exactly one** incident row until the chain verifies again — at which point a `audit.anchor.recovered` row closes it under the same correlation id.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keyed HMAC over the audit rows.** Rejected for the reason the Board gave for the control store: it adds a secret to custody and rotate, and it fails against an actor who can read the process. The external witness defends against a strictly larger class of attacker without a new secret.
- **A transparency log (RFC 6962 style) with a Merkle tree and inclusion proofs.** The right answer at scale and the natural successor: inclusion and consistency proofs would let a third party verify a single event without the whole chain. Rejected for now as disproportionate to dev/sim volumes and to the number of verifiers; the anchor record is deliberately shaped so a Merkle root can replace the head hash without changing the seam. [Open]
- **A public blockchain anchor.** Rejected: it publishes a fingerprint of internal activity timing to an uncontrolled audience, costs money per anchor, and the trust it adds over a WORM store written by a different principal is small for a private trading platform.
- **Signed timestamps from a TSA.** Complementary rather than alternative; it proves *when*, not *what was not removed*. Worth adding to the anchor record once a TSA exists. [Open]
- **Keep the JSONL file and back it up.** Rejected: a backup proves nothing about what was in the file before the backup ran, which is the OBJ-3 finding.

## Consequences

- The audit trail now survives a restart, and a restore of a stale backup is detectable rather than silent.
- Two directories must be provisioned, owned by two identities, and both must be in the DR plan. Who that second principal is in deployment stays O-54; the file publisher models it, it does not evidence it.
- Anchor records accumulate: one per explicit seal and one per `anchor_every` events. Compaction and retention are owed with the control-store journal's (O-111).
- A lost witness is now a fail-closed condition. That is the intended behaviour, and it is also a new way to stop a deployment: the runbook must say that restoring the replica, not disabling the check, is the fix.
- The cadence (`anchor_every`, `max_anchor_lag`) bounds how many events a consistent-rewrite attacker could reach and how stale the witness may be. Both defaults are dev/sim placeholders; the real values come from the O-54 decision and the retention rules. [Open]
- The Kill Switch auto-action on `audit.chain_verification_failed` means a broken chain halts the platform. That is deliberate and it is drilled by TC-AUD-007.
